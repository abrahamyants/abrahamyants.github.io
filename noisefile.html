The problem

Every city handles noise differently: what counts, who you call, what evidence they want, which form. People measure with a phone app, call the wrong line, and get nowhere. The rule exists, in the municipal code, and nobody reads it for them.

My part

All of it. I read the codes, built the rule set, wrote the Android app, drew the store listing, and shipped it on Google Play with billing. The legal reading is the part a lawyer brings: each rule is quoted from the city's current code on screen, with its section number, and the app never calls a phone reading a legal verdict. Tests enforce that boundary.

Key choices and why

Quote the code, never paraphrase it.
"THE CODE SAYS · Planning Code 17.120.050(G)(2)." The user can take that line to the city. Paraphrase would be my opinion; the quote is the law.
Local-first.
No account, no analytics, no ads. Measurements and the incident log stay on the phone. A complaint kit that phones home would be a different product.
One paid unlock.
$7.99 for the sealed PDF report, the city form guide, and the filled-in city email. Knowing the rule is free; the paperwork is the product.
Sources with hashes.
52 official sources archived with content hashes, so a rule can be traced to the exact page it came from on the day it was read.

What resulted

Production on Google Play since October 2026 under the name Neighbor Noise Complaint Kit, publisher WiM Labs. The listing was approved with the legal citations in it. First sale recorded. Work in progress: ratings and reach.
